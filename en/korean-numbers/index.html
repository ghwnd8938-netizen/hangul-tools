When to use which numbers

Korean has two number systems. Sino-Korean numbers (일, 이, 삼…) come from Chinese. Native Korean numbers (하나, 둘, 셋…): learn them from 1 to 99. For larger numbers, use Sino-Korean units such as 백 and 천; when counting, the last part may still be native.

  • Sino-Korean: prices (삼만 오천 원), phone numbers (read digit by digit; this tool reads 0 as 공, and 영 is also possible), years, months and days of the month (삼월 오 일), minutes and seconds (사십오 분), floors (삼 층, 3rd floor; 지하 일 층, B1), bus and room numbers, measurements, age with 세 on forms and in formal text (이십사 세), and the big units in large counts (백 개, 100 things).
  • Native Korean: counting things and people with a counter word (사과 세 개, three apples; 두 명, two people), the hour on the clock (세 시), age with 살 (스무 살), how many times (한 번) and durations in months (두 달).

Before a counter, five native numbers shorten: 하나 → 한, 둘 → 두, 셋 → 세, 넷 → 네, 스물 → 스무 (only for exactly 20; 21 is 스물한). Watch two month names: June is 유월 and October is 시월, not 육월 or 십월.

FAQ

Why is 10,000 just 만, but 100,000,000 is 일억?

The leading 1 is dropped before 십, 백, 천, and before 만 when it is the first unit: 만 원, 만 천 (11,000). 억 and 조 keep it: 일억, 일조. After a bigger unit this tool writes 일만 (일억 일만, 100,010,000) to avoid ambiguity. Plain 만 for 10,000 is this tool's convention, not the only correct form: 일만 is also seen, e.g. in banking or formal text, and cheques and contracts sometimes spell out 일만, 일천 to prevent tampering.

Why is 16 romanized simnyuk and not sipyuk?

십육 is pronounced [심뉵]: an ㄴ sound is added before 육 after a consonant, and ㅂ turns into ㅁ before ㄴ. The same happens in 백육 [뱅뉵], where ㄱ also becomes ㅇ. In 천육백 (1,600), said as one phrase, the added ㄴ is optional: both [천뉵빽] and [처뉵빽] are heard; this tool shows cheonnyukbaek. In 60 (육십) no ㄴ is added. 육십 is pronounced [육씹], but Revised Romanization doesn't show tensing: yuksip. 백만 sounds like [뱅만] and 십만 like [심만].

Is 3:30 세 시 삼십 분 or 세 시 반?

Both are correct. 반 (half) is very common in speech.

How do I say noon and midnight?

This tool treats 0:00–11:59 as 오전 (a.m.) and 12:00–23:59 as 오후 (p.m.), so 12:00 is 오후 열두 시 (정오, noon) and 0:00 is 오전 열두 시 (자정, midnight). In conversation people often say 낮 열두 시 and 밤 열두 시 instead. Readings like 십오 시 are heard in timetables and announcements.

Is 0 read 공 or 영 in phone numbers?

Both are used when digits are read one at a time: 011 can be 영일일 or 공일일. This tool uses 공 for 0 in phone numbers; 영 is also possible. As a quantity, zero is 영. Some speakers also say 에 at each dash.

Limitations

  • Whole numbers from 0 to 9,999,999,999,999,999 only. No negatives, decimals, fractions, ordinals (첫째) or dates.
  • Romanization reflects the standard pronunciation of each word. Tensing is not written (Revised Romanization does not mark it), sound changes across spaces are not shown (만 육천 is often said [만뉵천]), and casual speech may differ. Phone numbers are romanized digit by digit, with no sound changes.
  • Counter forms shown are the general ones. A few counters take other forms (석 달, 넉 잔), which are not covered.
  • The price reader understands Sino-Korean words and digits, not native numbers or English words.
